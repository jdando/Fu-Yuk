## 50. ENGAGEMENT SHAPE

**Pricing model.** Rubric: each criterion scored 1 (poor) to 5 (strong) for you, times weight. Max 50 pts.

```
CRITERION                     WEIGHT   T&M HOURLY   RETAINER + OVERAGE   FIXED FEE
income predictability         3        2            5                    3
scope-creep protection        3        4            3                    2
fit for advisory / SME work   2        4            5                    2
upside if you work fast       1        1            2                    5
low admin burden              1        3            4                    3
TOTAL                                  30 pts       40 pts               27 pts
```
Retainer = guaranteed block of hours per month at a small discount, with overage billed at TARGET. Fixed fee fits only a well-bounded deliverable (a report, a training package).

**Structure.** Not scored: the right answer depends on facts you don't have yet (their procurement rules, your liability exposure).

```
STRUCTURE                 YOU PAY            MISCLASSIFICATION RISK      FITS WHEN
1099, sole proprietor     both FICA halves   highest if same job,        short, small, one-off
                                             same boss, same hours
1099 to your own LLC      both FICA halves   lower; stronger with        ongoing work, more than one
                                             other clients, own tools    client likely
W-2 via staffing agency   employee half      lowest; agency is           client insists, or the work
                                             employer                    looks like the old job
```
Agency route: the agency's markup goes **on top of** your rate, not out of it. Your W-2 pay rate then compares against your old salary, not the 02 ladder.

Lens A: 65% conf retainer + overage beats T&M for SME work. Counter: a retainer invites "you're already paid, just answer this" creep → define the block in the SOW, track hours, bill overage monthly, no rollover beyond 1 mo.

Lens B: 55% conf LLC beats agency W-2. Counter: if the work is your old job with your old boss and hours, the contractor label fails the IRS and DOL tests no matter the paperwork → structure as deliverables, own schedule and tools; if they insist on fixed hours or supervision, take the agency route at a W-2 rate.

## 60. TALKING POINTS

**Value frame.** "You know me and I know your systems, people and history. Zero ramp-up." Cost of the alternative: recruiting plus ramp-to-competence for a replacement is `____` mo.

**Proof.** Your 3 wins from section 30, each in one sentence with an outcome.

**Why now (neutral, forward).** "I've set up an independent practice. Focused SME work like this is exactly what I'm doing."

**Availability.** "I can commit `____` h/mo starting `____`, once paperwork is signed."

**Price, if pressed.** "My rate for this kind of work is $`TARGET` to $`OPEN` per hour depending on scope and commitment. I'll put exact numbers in the proposal." State the range with **TARGET as the low end**; they will hear the low end.

## 70. QUESTIONS TO ASK THEM

```
PROBLEM
  10  What's the problem, in your words? Why now?

  30  Why me? Who else have you considered?

  40  What does done look like? What deliverables would you point to?
  50  Expected duration and hours per week or month?
  60  Advisory (answer questions) or hands-on (produce work)?

  70  Who is my point of contact? Who approves the spend?
  80  Who else is involved, and how do they feel about bringing me in?
LOGISTICS
  90  On-site, remote, or mixed? Travel?

MONEY AND PROCESS

```

## 100. LENS STRESS-TESTS

Lens C: 70% conf deferring price to a written proposal beats quoting on the call. Counter: they may need a number to get budget approved, and a refusal reads as evasive → give the TARGET-to-OPEN range verbally, exact numbers in writing within 48 h.

Lens D: 60% conf you hold more leverage than they do. Counter: they may have alternatives (other ex-employees, a consultancy, a new hire already in progress) → ask question 30 and read the urgency from question 20 before you anchor.
